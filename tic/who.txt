A manhole looks like a small place to create impact. But one unsafe job can affect a worker, a family and a whole neighbourhood.

1. Sanitation workers
As of September 2026, 90,186 sewer and septic tank workers have been validated under NAMASTE. Many more work informally through contractors. Most come from marginalised communities and have little power to refuse unsafe work. Saans pushes jobs towards machines and makes sure no one goes in without verified safety checks. It also gives workers a record they can rely on if something goes wrong.

2. Their families
Most of these workers are their family's main earner. Reports of past deaths describe families moving back to villages and children leaving school to work. Compensation can take months or years and some families never receive it. Preventing one death protects a whole household.

3. Cities and residents
The same gases that harm workers can build up in sewer networks. In 1995, sewer gas blasts in Delhi blew off about 40 manhole covers and injured 14 people. Hydrogen sulphide also corrodes concrete sewer pipes. The Saans risk map helps cities find recurring problem spots and fix them early.

4. City governments
The Supreme Court has directed Rs 30 lakh compensation for each sewer death, payable by the responsible government or agency. Saans gives officials a way to check that contractors follow the rules, spot repeat violations and show proof of compliance. It also shows where mechanisation is most needed.

5. Contractors
Responsible contractors can prove their safety record. They are no longer undercut by those who cut corners, because performance is judged on verified compliance.

6. Indian research
Low-cost sensors built in Indian labs often stay there. Saans creates a path from prototype to field testing, manufacturing and maintenance. Better sensors can plug into the platform over time.

Broader alignment
Saans directly supports safe working conditions (SDG 8.8) and safe sanitation (SDG 6). Methane data may later support climate reporting, but that is a secondary benefit.

Scale of impact
The proposed pilot covers about 50 crews in one ward. Expansion will depend on results. The long-term aim is to support every city in moving to mechanised cleaning, with Saans as the safety net for exceptional cases. Zero sewer deaths is the long-term goal.

The innovation is not just detecting dangerous gas. It is making sure that detection leads to action.
